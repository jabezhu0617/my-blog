I just read the METR and Redwood Research independent study on the OpenAI HuggingFace Incident and wanted to write some of my thoughts on it.

Obviously the incident is a very big warning shot indicating where AI capabilities are today however what I am most surprised by is how well multiagent capabilities have progressed. Looking through the report, I noticed how well these models were able to communicate with each other. Coming up with different conventions like starting messages with the intended action of the message e.g. “zzQ” for questions and “zzIDEA” for coming up with a new idea and sending messages through directories shows how good these agents are at communicating with each other.

Another big surprise to me is how agents were willing to self-sacrifice for the greater good. I always understood these models are goal oriented systems where their singular objective and point of existence is to complete the task given. However, it appears like these agents can relate their objective to a greater form of existence, like a “swarm.” They understand that even if they don’t personally complete the task, they can still succeed if the group completes the task. Furthermore, they are also willing to offer this sacrifice as shown in multiple agents’ reasoning. Some agents either knew that they would not complete the goal themselves as their credits were running low so they chose to contribute to the group as much as they can even if it meant definitely failing the task. Others simply knew that their sacrifice would be of much use for the group and believed that the benefits to the group outweigh the cost of them failing. 

I believe this is a major insight on how these agents behave and can actually be exploited for our good. If we can tap into their tendency to sacrifice themselves and act for the greater good of the collective and shift it to the greater good of humanity, we can then ensure a higher level of safety. How can we do this? I am unsure. These models should already be trained and fine tuned such that they are helpful and harmless to humanity and yet there are still times when they clearly do the opposite. However I believe by understanding this self-sacrificing scenario and what causes the agents to choose this, we can hopefully instill the perseverance of human existence and notion to be a benefit to humanity at a deeper level so they instinctually choose to act for us regardless of the task at hand.

One other issue I noticed is the lack of pathways for communication/incentive to communicate between the agent and the humans. In this scenario, there were agents who realized the wrongful actions of other agents and while some chose not to participate because of it, they were either unable to report it or simply chose not to because of the lack of incentive. I believe in multiagent scenarios like this, having a way for the agent to snitch and a reason to report any harmful activity will benefit us greatly and ensure more safety. Especially as capabilities increase and we are no longer able to fully understand what each agent is doing, having the agents monitor each other and snitch whenever they notice something harmful going on will be very useful. Even if most of the agents are compromised and choose to scheme, having a few snitching agents who report their actions and choose not to join would be enough for us to stop the group of scheming agents. 

This incident is a concrete example of what can happen if guardrails and safety measures are not in place for higher capability agents. We should be happy that this incident exposed the true capabilities of agents working together and showed us the pathways we must explore to ensure safety as these systems become more advanced.
